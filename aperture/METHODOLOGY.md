## Mission, boundary and invariant principles

APERTURE converts a real company's verified problem into a measurable, low-risk, accountable operating improvement. It is a **methodology for doing business work**, not a dashboard, a decorative prototype, or evidence of an employer relationship. It applies to fractional operations, franchise field performance, GTM and RevOps, program delivery, youth development and scientific partnership coordination, with domain experts responsible for licensed/regulatory decisions.

Non-negotiable: truth before narrative; evidence before assertion; dependencies before deadlines; reversible action before automation; human approval before externally consequential actions; process validity as well as outcome validity; no invented clients, units, endorsements, qualifications, job vacancies, results, or agent activity.

## One compact delivery system: PROVE

1. **P — Pin the reality.** Check the official posting/customer mandate, current business baseline, approved source documents, constraints, ownership and conflicts. Record source URL, date, version, evidence grade and unknowns. A benchmark paper is a method inspiration, not evidence of a specific client's problem.
2. **R — Reason across dependencies.** Construct the smallest useful dependency graph; list upstream information, accountable humans, acceptance rules, reversible actions and approvals. Expand plans after evidence comes in, not ahead of it.
3. **O — Operate one controlled improvement.** Choose the smallest commercially meaningful intervention; agree an owner, deadlines, leading indicator, success threshold, rollback rule and authorization. Avoid automating a broken process.
4. **V — Verify the entire execution.** Test intermediate steps, approvals, protected state, final output, negative controls, latency and cost. Repeat tests across realistic variations; an attractive final output cannot excuse invalid steps.
5. **E — Evolve from results.** Compare to a real baseline, document what changed, distinguish causation from correlation, retire ineffective steps, and update the playbook and evidence register. Carry forward only demonstrated improvements.

## Embedded business plan — seven blocks that belong in every serious proposal

**1. Opportunity / mandate.** One sentence identifying the organization's explicit problem, sourced to the current posting or confirmed buyer brief; separate company facts from working hypotheses.

**2. Baseline and economics.** Identify current revenue or service model, customer segment, bottleneck, controllable cost, and the three metrics that would credibly reveal improvement. Don't use claimed employer numbers without permission. Give formulas, not invented forecasts.

**3. Business diagnosis.** A working thesis, at least two alternative explanations, root causes to test, and the critical dependencies/decision rights.

**4. Day 0–30: Discover and validate.** Interview stakeholders, reconstruct the current journey, select a baseline, check policies, identify bottlenecks, propose priority change. Deliverable: signed/approved problem specification, action ledger and baseline.

**5. Day 31–60: Implement and learn.** Run one bounded intervention, set owner and rollback, document changes and customer effects. Deliverable: usable SOP, staff coaching, weekly result note, compliance checks.

**6. Day 61–90: Scale only validated practice.** Expand to additional units/accounts only when observed impact supports it. Deliverable: playbook, risk register, ROI range with assumptions, training handoff and next 90-day decision.

**7. Commercial terms and trust.** Scope, weekly availability, deliverables, owner approvals, confidentiality and data boundaries, invoicing, termination and IP, no requirement to buy a dashboard. Pricing is a proposed negotiation, not an accepted contract.

## Research refresh and quality gates

- Monthly and before any new outreach batch: query each relevant research source, deduplicate titles/IDs, retain publication versions and dates, inspect paper abstract, original record, venue decision when available, code/results and limitations. Select at most ten *relevant* verified studies per role; never imply all feeds produced a study.
- Eligibility: link works, source identity consistent, ≤ 90 days when claiming “in the last months,” no withdrawn or superseded result presented as current, clear transfer mechanism, measurable proposed test.
- Deployment: outreach must be an independently reviewable **complete business proposal in the email body** with direct source links. Do not require a website click, dashboard, sign-in, or call to understand the offer.
- Production verification: compare claimed completed actions with Git commits, build tests, live site, Gmail Sent receipts and ATS receipts as separate statuses. Reject fabricated sending or client results.
- Financial: compute gross contribution as *incremental revenue × contribution margin − incremental operating cost* (not revenue alone); distinguish upside assumptions and downside.
- Acceptance: every proposal includes buyer-specific problem, experience match with honest gaps, specific 30/60/90 tasks, outcomes, source-backed rationale, commercial next step, and consent before email sends.

## Engagement business model

Offer three clear shapes for **negotiation, not advertised prices**:
- **Diagnostic sprint**: 1–2 weeks, limited interviews and source review, baseline and prioritized plan, fixed scoped fee.
- **90-day fractional operator**: typically 10–20 hours/week, agreed hourly or monthly retainer, weekly evidence-based milestones; no commitment until scope and terms agreed.
- **Multi-unit implementation**: after validating first-unit performance, separately priced rollout per additional unit/site/network with training and quality assurance.

For each calculate buyer value from actual baseline: net incremental contribution, reduction in rework cost, saved staff time, retention lift, and value at risk. Never promise a particular ROI absent verified data.
