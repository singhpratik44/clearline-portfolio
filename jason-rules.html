CLEARLINE / APERTURE · JASON RULES v1

Search for better representations. Prove the useful ones.

A disciplined research-and-decision protocol inspired by reversible representation work. It makes the system more intelligent by improving the question and the representation before spending more inference, simulation, or human attention.

The operating rules

1
Preserve the object.

Never optimize a display, prompt, or score while silently changing the underlying decision object. Keep identity, assumptions, dependencies, and authority intact.

2
Search representation before complexity.

Try a clearer state encoding, dependency graph, invariant, or decomposition before adding a larger model or more agents.

3
Require reversible transformations.

Every transformation must have a deterministic inverse, a round-trip test, or an explicit loss certificate. No hidden information loss.

4
Separate equivalence from improvement.

A new representation may be easier to compute without being more truthful. Test semantic equivalence first; claim improvement only against a baseline.

5
Use the cheapest sufficient route.

Route routine work to deterministic rules, ambiguity to targeted agents, complex tradeoffs to bounded simulation, and consequential actions to authorized humans.

6
Spend compute where uncertainty is causal.

Do not ask an agent to reason about a downstream symptom when a prerequisite, capacity limit, or missing fact explains it.

7
Make failure informative.

Every failed proposal must identify the violated invariant, missing evidence, affected dependencies, and smallest recoverable next experiment.

8
Bound the search.

Set budgets for time, tokens, simulations, branches, and risk. Stop when the marginal evidence is lower than the cost.

9
Never let exploration mutate truth.

Agents and simulations produce proposals, patches, and evidence. Only the kernel can update canonical state, and only after authorization.

10
Publish uncertainty honestly.

Label theory, simulation evidence, operational evidence, and hardware demonstration separately. Never convert an interesting result into an unsupported claim.

Route a decision

This evaluator is deterministic. It demonstrates the rule: complexity and uncertainty choose the method; consequence chooses the approval gate.

Proof obligations

Before accepting a new representation, run:

  • canonicalization is deterministic;
  • round-trip output is preserved;
  • baseline and new method use the same inputs;
  • constraints and authorization remain unchanged;
  • failure and rollback paths are recorded;
  • claims match the evidence level.

Jason/QIH is experimental conventional computation. This page does not claim quantum hardware, quantum advantage, or a formal proof of a physical theory.
