# CODE THE GAME — Teacher Pilot Pack v1
Status: draft for educator review, not district-approved or independently classroom-tested.

## Audience and prerequisites
Suggested grades 6–8, one 45–60-minute session weekly for six weeks. Browser-only activities require a modern browser and keyboard or touch input. Optional Roblox Studio, Godot 4 and Minecraft Education pathways require separately approved devices, software, accounts and licenses. Do not require student email, account creation or payment for the free lab.

## Outcomes and evidence
Students will predict movement, represent position, define and vary a numerical parameter, conduct controlled tests, revise a design for a peer, and communicate conclusions with quantitative evidence. Evidence: prediction, coordinates table, test comparison, revision notes and final explanation. Do not collect names or sensitive student information in the browser activity.

## Six teacher-led sessions
| Week | Goal | Teacher launch (10 min) | Student work (25–35 min) | Exit ticket (5–10 min) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Predict and test | Demonstrate the free movement game | Predict route, play, compare move count | What differed from your prediction? |
| 2 | Represent position | Model x/y coordinates on paper | Log five positions; calculate horizontal/vertical displacement | Explain one coordinate change |
| 3 | Use variables | Define movement step as a quantity | Test step sizes 10 and 30; compare number of moves | Which setting worked better and why? |
| 4 | Design for others | Discuss access, clarity and feedback | Sketch a new challenge; peer usability review | Name one accessibility improvement |
| 5 | Investigate and revise | Model a fair test with one variable | Repeat trials, record outcomes, revise strategy | What evidence changed your decision? |
| 6 | Explain and present | Model claim–evidence–reasoning | Present game design decision and coordinate/variable evidence | Submit reflection |

## Teacher facilitation and answer guidance
- Week 1: Different move counts are acceptable if the route or step size differs; assess whether prediction and observation are distinguished.
- Week 2: For a right move of 20 pixels, x increases by 20 and y remains unchanged. The browser uses nonnegative screen coordinates; negative numbers and four quadrants require a separate paper-grid extension.
- Week 3: A step size is a variable controlling displacement per input. Larger steps can overshoot the goal, so fewer moves is not always better.
- Week 4: Accept designs that describe concrete learner needs, such as visible focus, readable instructions, keyboard access, or reduced motion.
- Week 5: A valid comparison holds starting position, goal, and route constant while varying one input; record trials rather than claiming statistical significance.
- Week 6: Strong explanations state a claim, cite at least two observations, and describe a trade-off or limitation.

## Rubric (0–3 per dimension, total 12)
| Dimension | 3 — strong | 2 — developing | 1 — emerging | 0 — absent |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prediction and measurement | Predicts, measures, and compares accurately | Predicts and measures with minor gaps | Partial observation | No evidence |
| Mathematical representation | Correctly explains positions and variable effects | Mostly correct with small errors | Incomplete or confused | No representation |
| Iteration and inclusion | Tests a revision and explains user impact | Revises with some rationale | Revision lacks rationale | No revision |
| Communication | Clear claim with two observations and limitations | Clear claim with some evidence | Claim with weak evidence | No explanation |

## Protecting the Center decision routine
1. Identify the learner and the goal being protected.
2. Ask whose access, safety or understanding might be affected.
3. Make a reversible change to one game parameter.
4. Record what happened and compare to prediction.
5. Keep or undo the change based on evidence.
