# CODE THE GAME — Robotics & Hackathon Team Kit (Draft v1)
Updated November 12, 2026. This is an independent preparation resource, not an official competition rulebook, team registration, league affiliation, or guarantee of eligibility.

## Before forming a team
1. Select a competition using the official organizer links on [the classroom hub](./#competitions).
2. Verify the exact season, regional organizer, age/grade limits, team size, mentor/guardian rules, fees, hardware, safety policies and accessibility provisions **directly with the organizer**.
3. Decide whether your team is entering robotics, app/AI, or hackathon tracks. Do not purchase specialized hardware until eligibility and requirements are confirmed.
4. Obtain all necessary guardian, school and district permissions. Never publish children's personally identifiable information, photos or private work without authorization.
5. Choose a concrete problem, measurable outcome, and feasible demonstration.

## The Protecting the Center competition routine
- **Center:** What student, community or problem are we protecting or helping?
- **Evidence:** What observation supports the problem?
- **Options:** Name two approaches and their constraints.
- **Reversible test:** Prototype the cheapest safe version first.
- **Review:** Record failures, revise and cite evidence; keep the people affected in view.
Framework: https://protecting-the-center.netlify.app/ — independent project, not an organizer requirement.

## Suggested six-session preparation sprint
| Session | Team task | Evidence and deliverable |
|---|---|---|
| 1: Understand | Read official rules together; write a problem statement | Eligibility checklist + source links |
| 2: Design | Sketch alternative strategies and roles | Two concepts + selection criteria |
| 3: Prototype | Make a tabletop model, browser simulation or coded demo | Working proof-of-concept + source/version |
| 4: Test | Run at least three repeatable trials; record errors | Test table, failed cases, adjustments |
| 5: Improve | Change one variable; document iteration and limitations | Before/after comparison + safety review |
| 6: Present | Practice a 2-minute demo and honest Q&A | Demo, explanation, independent documentation |

## Roles for a small team
- Builder: models mechanical or interface solution.
- Programmer: implements and documents code.
- Tester: creates fair trials and reproduces errors.
- Researcher: validates rules, references and user needs.
- Presenter: explains impact, decisions and limitations.
Students may rotate roles; never require a particular identity, device or prior experience.

## Independent team assessment (0–3 each / 15 total)
| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Research and constraints | No sources | Incomplete | Mostly verified | Current official rules fully cross-checked |
| Computational model | Missing | Basic idea | Working partial demo | Working testable implementation |
| Testing and iteration | No test | One anecdote | Repeatable tests | Repeatable tests with documented revision |
| Human-centered impact | Missing | Stated vaguely | Realistic user need | Need, access and safety meaningfully addressed |
| Evidence-based presentation | Missing | Unclear | Credible claims | Clear demo, quantified evidence and limitations |

This is a classroom rubric only. Each external organizer has its **own official judging rubric**.

## Hardware / software realities
- **LEGO Education Computer Science & AI** is a separate hardware product. CODE THE GAME does not sell or bundle LEGO kits. The new LEGO League uses specified approved equipment; see official rules.
- **SPIKE** products are retired from direct LEGO Education sales as of June 30, 2026, but the SPIKE App remains supported to June 30, 2031. Verify season-specific competition use.
- **VEX/WRO** competition hardware, fields, registration and fees are independent purchases/rules.
- **NASA/hackathons** may have local age, consent and venue rules. Do not claim school or minors' participation without required approvals.
- **Computer vision/AI**: the website's classification demo is a deterministic threshold rule, *not* a trained machine-learning model.
