How to begin

Pick the prompt that fits today. Write a sentence, draw a small sketch, or answer quietly to yourself. Pause at any time. The purpose is to notice, not to make yourself feel a certain way.

Five free prompts

1. What am I carrying right now?

Describe one thought or feeling without debating whether it is reasonable.

Try: "Right now, I notice..."

2. What's a fact, and what's a story?

Write one directly observable fact. Then label the interpretation you're making about it as a thought, not a certainty.

Try: "I know ___ happened; I'm thinking ___ might mean ___."

3. What can I actually influence today?

Name one small step within your control. It can be as ordinary as drinking water, asking a question, or taking a short break.

Try: "One action I can take is..."

4. What support would be helpful?

Think about the difference between what you can do alone and what would be easier with trusted support.

Try: "I could ask ___ for ___."

5. What do I want to remember tomorrow?

Finish with a reminder that is realistic rather than overly positive.

Try: "One thing worth remembering is..."

A gentle routine in five minutes

Minute one: look around and notice three things you can see. Minute two: name the feeling or concern on your mind. Minutes three and four: answer one prompt above in writing or aloud. Minute five: choose a manageable next action and intentionally stop.

There's no need to count your breathing or close your eyes. If a practice causes discomfort, skip it. If journaling brings up something serious or distressing, a qualified professional or trusted support person may be more helpful than another prompt.

Do I need to journal every day?

No. If five minutes helps, do five. If one sentence helps, do one. If today is not the day for reflection, that is okay too. A journal is a tool, not a measure of your worth.

Can journaling replace therapy?

No. These are general-purpose reflection prompts. They don't diagnose or treat mental health conditions and aren't crisis support. For urgent emotional distress in the United States, you can call or text 988. For immediate danger, contact emergency services.
