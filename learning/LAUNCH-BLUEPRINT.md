# APERTURE Learning — specialization-led go-to-market and growth plan
*Working strategy, November 2026. No unverified claims or invented partners.*

## Product story and promise

**The customer story:** *My child loves something → we discover a specialization → we try a real interaction → a human coach helps build a project → we see concrete progress → the student presents something original → the family chooses the next chapter.*

**Primary value proposition:** Live, personalized, project-based coding and AI-literacy education in which families can understand what the student worked on, what skill they demonstrated and what comes next.

**Brand system:** "Every child has a world to create." The five worlds are Creative Worlds, Code Builders, AI Frontiers, Engineering & Systems, and Real-World Creators. Each has four initial specializations, for 20 tracks. These are *learning directions*, not twenty staffed courses or claims of immediate availability.

**Explicit commercial promise:** $65 purchases one live, 60-minute private online lesson (once). Multiple Stripe quantity units purchase multiple lessons, if quantity is enabled. Recurring weekly billing is **not yet active**—it requires a separate Stripe recurring price/link and clear scheduling, cancellation, missed-session and refund terms. School contracts, after-school clubs and cohort tuition are quoted separately.

## Three rounds of strategy improvement

### Round 1 — stop selling generic coding classes
Show a *specific future-oriented question* for each prospect: "Does your child want to build a Roblox game, an AI experiment, a basketball shot chart, or a useful app?" Provide a visible specialization, a clear first project, a coach interaction and an honest price.

### Round 2 — give the family a working taste
The customer should see a real interactive demo before buying. The website already has:
- A searchable, filterable 20-specialization atlas with four learning chapters and copyable roadmaps.
- A project studio with arcade, branching narrative and simple AI classifier.
- A learning studio with a customizable pathway, working toy game, sample progress report and tuition explorer.
- A live one-time Stripe buy button for the $65 session.

Do not present sample progress reports or demos as actual student work.

### Round 3 — build a measurable, trust-led flywheel
A verified coach delivers an appropriate lesson; the family receives a tangible progress recap; guardian-approved projects and authentic feedback build proof; positive word of mouth feeds the next cohort. In addition to conversion rate, evaluate the **quality of fulfilled lessons** and repeat enrollment.

## Where to market — order for a lean budget

### 1. Former colleagues, trusted coaches and referrals
**Why first:** Warm credibility and staffing quality can be established before paid acquisition. Contact former coaches individually, ask for optional availability and specializations, and agree on compensation, consent and child safeguarding. Ask permission before referrals or sharing names. Do not describe them as employed until they accept.

**Offer:** "Help us build the next generation of project-based coding instruction: real projects, responsible AI, flexible online classes." Aim to verify two to three instructor options in distinct tracks rather than claiming all twenty are staffed.

### 2. Orange County parent organizations / PTAs
Request permission to contribute to a school PTA newsletter or host a free learning showcase; do not imply PTA or district endorsement. The Fourth District PTA has an official public directory of units and councils, including Orange Unified, Santa Ana, Tustin and Irvine-area networks: https://fourthdistrictpta.org/units/ . Use the council directory to identify appropriate organizers, not to scrape parent contacts or send bulk unsolicited mail.

**Offer:** A free 20–30-minute "Build a Mini Game" or "How AI Makes Mistakes" parent-child demonstration that links to the corresponding catalog specialty.

### 3. Nextdoor business posts
Create/claim an accurate APERTURE Learning business page and share a real demo or parent resource with local adults. Nextdoor documents free business pages and posts: https://business.nextdoor.com/en-us/getting-started/business-page . Moderate responses and avoid posting child data. Avoid claims of a physical storefront when this is online instruction.

**Offer:** "Which project would your child build? Explore 20 specializations, try a demo free."

### 4. Permission-based parent communities and homeschool groups
Find active Orange County homeschool, local parent and neighborhood communities; read rules, contact admins, and provide an educational resource rather than repetitive advertising. Do not scrape group rosters, private data or child profiles.

**Offer:** Free interactive specialization finder plus a short parent curriculum FAQ.

### 5. School/youth-program partnership outreach
Contact program directors and administrators about after-school enrichment and short-format workshops, **not** a generic blast to teachers. Include one page: goals, intended ages, example projects, instructor qualifications (once verified), child safety policies, operational scope, duration and quoted budget.

**Offer:** Pilot workshop/proposal on a specific interest area, subject to procurement, staffing and safeguarding approvals.

### 6. Search/SEO and helpful short videos
Publish specific, age-appropriate content answering parent queries: "Roblox coding lessons online," "Python for middle school students," "Scratch animation first project," "how to teach AI literacy responsibly," "basketball analytics coding project." Film screen captures of original demo code, not students without consent. Attach the corresponding specialization deep link and a visible $65 offer.

### 7. Paid acquisition only after the funnel converts
Use a very small test budget only if instructor capacity is reliable, checkout works, customer contact and fulfillment are operational, and at least one genuine conversion is measured. Aim parent-facing channels at adults. Avoid targeting children as advertising prospects.

**Google Business Profile caveat:** Google explicitly excludes online-only businesses without qualifying face-to-face interaction. Do **not** create an ineligible or fictitious Santa Ana storefront simply for rankings: https://support.google.com/business/answer/7039811

## Campaign concepts and specialization-specific landing messages

- **Roblox:** "Your child plays Roblox. What would happen if they designed their own game logic?"
- **Python:** "From 'Hello, World' to a program that actually does something."
- **AI:** "Don't just prompt AI—understand why it can be wrong."
- **Sports:** "Love basketball? Turn game observations into a real analytics project."
- **Robotics:** "From a moving robot idea to sensors, decisions and tested control logic."
- **Creative coding:** "Let their favorite story become an interactive world."

Each campaign should resolve into the same 6-step journey: **ad / referral → track deep link → four milestones → working demo → coaching inquiry / Stripe price → fulfilled lesson and parent recap**.

## Testable 30-day launch

**Week 1:** Confirm two or more instructors and schedules. Establish explicit child protection, privacy, scope of service, tax, payment and refund policies. Test the Stripe live checkout path without actually charging a customer. Publish clear scheduling and response expectations.

**Week 2:** Send permission-based coach and partnership introductions. Create five parent-facing demo videos mapped to five specializations. Publish one simple parent FAQ for safety, learning outcomes and pricing.

**Week 3:** Contact suitable PTA/community organizers for a complimentary workshop. Post useful demonstrations where permitted. Create an intake/booking process and standard guardian communication.

**Week 4:** Run the first workshop if approved and feasible; measure qualified parent inquiries, paid sessions, fulfillment, learner milestone reports, repeat bookings, referrals and instructor capacity. Review the bottleneck: discoverability, trust, scheduling or price. Improve only the highest-friction step.

## Scorecard and ethical measurement

| Stage | Signal | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| Reach | Content and referral views | Aggregate adult-facing views or community attendance |
| Interest | Atlas entries | Visits reaching the specialization catalog |
| Intent | Track explorations | Track selected and roadmap viewed (privacy-respecting aggregate, not child profiling) |
| Inquiry | Qualified guardian conversations | Parent or authorized program contact requests real next steps |
| Sale | Paid sessions | Verified, authorized Stripe payments |
| Fulfillment | Sessions delivered | Lesson completed with agreed scope and documented parent summary |
| Value | Repeat purchases / attendance | Distinguish genuine repeated purchases from auto-renewals |
| Trust | Permission-based feedback | Authentic parent testimonials and partner references only |

Do not install third-party analytics targeting children or use sensitive student data for marketing. Privacy, consent and child-safety protections are part of the product.

## Why this can win without inflated claims

A generic coding tutor offers an hour. A well-run APERTURE pathway offers **a deliberately chosen interest, a concrete first project, a teacher who can help, an evidence-based progression and an honest continuation decision**. The differentiation must be proven in delivery, not merely asserted in branding.
