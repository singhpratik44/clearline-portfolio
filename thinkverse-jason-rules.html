The Thinkverse Jason Rules

1. Start with the district outcome.

Frame every conversation around a measurable learning or teaching problem—not around AI novelty.

2. Map the decision system.

Identify the superintendent, curriculum leader, math lead, teacher, student, family, IT, procurement, and budget dependencies before proposing a pilot.

3. Preserve educator agency.

Represent Thinkverse as an AI co-teacher that supports professional judgment, not an opaque replacement for teachers.

4. Make the pilot reversible.

Define a bounded cohort, baseline, success measure, privacy boundary, support plan, exit condition, and expansion gate before launch.

5. Separate signal from story.

Keep student outcomes, usage, teacher feedback, confidence, and implementation evidence distinct. Never turn engagement into proficiency without proof.

6. Route the real bottleneck.

If adoption is blocked by training, integration, procurement, or trust, do not respond with more product features or more outreach.

7. Use the cheapest sufficient experiment.

Test the smallest district-relevant hypothesis that can change the next decision, then expand only when evidence and authority align.

8. Convert every objection into a dependency.

“Teachers are busy,” “IT needs review,” and “we need budget” become explicit owners, prerequisites, evidence, and next actions.

9. Keep agents proposal-only.

AI may summarize, identify patterns, and propose next steps; district commitments, claims, and consequential actions require human approval.

10. Scale only what survives review.

Expansion requires reproducible evidence, educator acceptance, operational readiness, and a clear rollback path.
