01 / The journey

How I found my way to the sideline.

Basketball gave me a language of teamwork, pressure, and belonging. Education taught me how to build the conditions in which young people can grow. Coaching brings those worlds together.

The beginning

Learning the game.

I played basketball through high school. What stayed with me was more than technique: preparation, teammates, setbacks, and the responsibility to keep showing up.

2021–2025

Learning to teach.

At theCoderSchool and Code Ninjas, I led youth education programs, supported instructors, coordinated families, and saw how confidence grows through patient, structured instruction.

2026 / Orange County

Coaching on the court.

As a youth basketball coach with SoCal Elite Sports, I focus on fundamentals, sportsmanship, clear expectations, positive reinforcement, and accountable effort.

The road ahead

Making growth visible.

I am developing APERTURE coaching prototypes to help plan practices, organize observations, and reflect on how to serve every player better. This is ongoing work, not a claim of team deployment.

“A good coach does not simply demand excellence. A good coach makes it possible for someone else to discover their own.”
02 / Faith into action

Two sacred traditions.
One daily obligation to serve.

I draw personal coaching lessons from Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji and the Holy Bible. Their traditions have distinct theological teachings; I approach them with respect, not as interchangeable texts. My question is practical: how can these readings make me a more humble, patient, and useful coach?

Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji · Japji Sahib · Ang 1
ਹੁਕਮਿ ਰਜਾਈ ਚਲਣਾ ਨਾਨਕ ਲਿਖਿਆ ਨਾਲਿ ॥੧॥

“Hukam rajaa-ee chalnaa, Nanak likhiaa naal.” A teaching about walking in accordance with divine Hukam. In my coaching reflection: accept the moment before you respond; govern your conduct rather than trying to control every outcome.

The Holy Bible · Proverbs 27:17 · KJV
“Iron sharpeneth iron; so a man sharpeneth the countenance of his friend.”

My coaching reflection: teammates and mentors refine one another through honest challenge, encouragement, and correction offered without humiliation.

01 / SERVICE

Seva

The quiet work matters: the struggling player, the equipment, the listening, the extra explanation.

Dialogue: Mark 10:45 (Bible)
02 / HUMILITY

Nimrata

Correction begins with me. A coach should be teachable, especially when a plan does not work.

Dialogue: Philippians 2:3–4 (Bible)
03 / TRUTH

Sach

Give accurate feedback. Say what you saw, distinguish it from what you assumed, and invite a second attempt.

Dialogue: Ephesians 4:25 (Bible)
04 / PERSEVERANCE

Steady courage

A mistake is information, not identity. Keep the standard high and the player's dignity intact.

Dialogue: James 1:2–4 (Bible)

The four coaching applications above are my interpretations, not direct scripture quotations or official Sikh/Christian doctrinal statements. Seva, nimrata, and sach are Sikh ethical concepts; biblical references are presented as a respectful second conversation with them.

03 / APERTURE × coaching

Compassion is the standard.
Evidence is how I improve.

APERTURE is my independent operating framework. In coaching, it turns good intentions into a repeatable cycle without reducing children to numbers.

01

Observe

Watch the skill, the confidence, and the context. Describe behavior without labeling the child.

02

Interpret

Separate what I saw from my hypothesis about why it happened.

03

Teach

Give one clear cue. Design a progression where a player can safely try again.

04

Recheck

Revisit the skill and ask the player how the adjustment felt.

05

Reflect

Record what changed, what remains uncertain, and the next coaching action.

◈

APERTURE integrity rule: A coach retains judgment and responsibility. No automated assessment of minors, no private athlete records, no unsupported improvement claims. This public website offers illustrative practice planning only.
