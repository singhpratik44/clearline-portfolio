Article V — Program view (critical path on the decision DAG)

Every initiative is a node; dependencies are edges. The engine runs forward/backward passes over the DAG — earliest start, latest start, slack — and the critical path (longest path) is the sequence where any delay moves the whole network. Same graph theory as the QEC decoder's matching graph, pointed at schedule instead of errors.

FDD clocks are hard constraints pinned to the calendar (no slack by law); support plans and expansion commits float until they land on the critical path. Delay a node and watch the path re-route — the system re-sequences work instead of discovering the slip at quarter-end. (Initiative seed shown for the franchise pack; each domain pack ships its own initiative DAG.)

Article VI — Decision nexus (commit drawer)

Article XI — Network twin (forecast self-calibration)

  

    

      
      
    

    

    

      
Each unit carries a forecast class — rising, holding, or deteriorating — derived deterministically from its own control variables (τ breach, critical distance vs. the τ = 2.1 wk / $0.8k thresholds), plus a stated confidence. The twin then scores the forecast against realized health from the same canonical state:

      
Forecast classHit condition (realized health)

        
risinghealth ≥ 70

        
deterioratinghealth < 60

        
holding55 ≤ health ≤ 80

      

      
Calibration is earned, not asserted. The engine publishes its forward-classification accuracy and lets the record — not the rhetoric — argue for trust. A class that keeps missing is visible here before anyone acts on it.
