THE TURN.

A 90-second, original fictional basketball breakdown. What looks like a difficult jumper is really a chain of small decisions.

Set-up: A right-handed player catches at the elbow. The on-ball defender crowds the middle; a helper waits at the rim. The player must create space without pushing off.

01 / SEE

READ THE FEET

Before the dribble, identify the defender's top foot and the helper's position. The open space is not always the obvious lane.

02 / CREATE

PIVOT WITH PURPOSE

Use a controlled plant and shoulder turn. Create separation through body position—not an offensive foul.

03 / FINISH

LAND IN BALANCE

The shot ends with a stable release and a quick transition back to defense. Footwork is the skill.

THREE QUESTIONS TO COACH

  1. Which defender position makes the turnaround a good or poor choice?
  2. If help arrives early, which pass is better than forcing the shot?
  3. Where should the player land to recover for transition defense?

Try it: recreate this fictional sequence with three cones or paper markers. Pause after each decision and explain one alternative. This study is for general education, not an official NBA moment.
