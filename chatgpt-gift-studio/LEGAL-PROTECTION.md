# Gift Finder — Legal Protection & Brand Risk Register
Prepared 2026-11-12. Operational checklist, not legal advice, trademark clearance, registration, or legal opinion.

## Immediate red flag: name is descriptive and crowded
"Gift Finder" directly describes the service, and "Gift Giver" is also used by existing products. A historical PRICELESS GIFT FINDER registration expressly disclaimed "GIFT FINDER" (registration 3790238, later cancelled). Current competing services include giftgiverapp.com and Gift Finder AI. These are preliminary public-web findings, not an exhaustive live USPTO, state, common-law, app-store or domain clearance. **Do not claim exclusive ownership of the phrase "Gift Finder" or that it is available for federal registration.**

## Brand architecture
- Keep **Gift Finder** as a plain-language feature/category for discoverability.
- Select and clear a **distinctive source-identifying house brand** before large marketing spend or federal trademark filing. Candidate names require clearance; APERTURE and CLEARLINE themselves are not assumed clear.
- Once cleared, use the brand consistently in the app, logo, merchant contracts, support, receipts and social accounts. Do not use ® before federal registration. TM can indicate a claim but does not confer registration.

## Trademark clearance and filing
1. Search exact, phonetic and visually similar names at https://tmsearch.uspto.gov/ across related services (online gift recommendation, downloadable software, gift retail/referrals, digital gift creation). Search live and relevant dead records, plus state registers, Google, app stores, domains and social handles.
2. Have a qualified US trademark lawyer review confusing similarity, descriptiveness, ownership and classifications. USPTO guidance: https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/federal-trademark-searching
3. Identify true owner (individual vs existing legal entity), its actual formation and authority. Never assume a brand is a registered company.
4. Determine actual use-in-commerce vs bona fide intent-to-use separately for each listed service. Preserve dated, unaltered website screenshots, first sales/invoices, app pages, public release evidence, and source history. Do not invent first-use dates.
5. If cleared, file the distinctive **standard-character word mark** first when appropriate; then consider logo. File through https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/apply and budget filing/attorney fees only after owner approval.
6. Calendar prosecution and renewal deadlines. Monitor confusing uses, but do not threaten others without counsel.

## Copyright, code and ownership
1. Preserve Git commit history, release tags, authored design files, licenses and creation dates.
2. Audit npm/open-source licenses and attribution. Confirm contractor assignments and any external creative rights in writing.
3. Copyright may protect original source code, art and text, not the abstract idea of a gift finder or generic features. Review registration at https://www.copyright.gov/registration/.
4. Move sensitive business logic and private keys out of public repos. Do not store production secrets in git.
5. Put contributor IP assignment, confidentiality, work-for-hire where applicable, and deliverables ownership into contractor agreements.

## Consumer, privacy and commerce
1. Public terms and privacy policy must accurately describe data processing, free finder, paid goods, affiliate commissions, cancellations, refunds, recipient links, retention and contact channels.
2. Avoid sensitive recipient data; don't expose private messages through public logs or analytics. Consider whether children will use the app and age-gate or avoid collecting children's data without compliance review.
3. If using affiliate links, disclose material connections clearly near recommendations; ensure recommendations aren't falsely described as unbiased if sponsored.
4. Make paid checkout and fulfillment honest: verify payment server-side, secure gift access, deliver promised assets, document refund/support policies, review sales tax, accessibility, and cross-border sales.
5. Obtain consent before marketing emails or texts. Avoid unsolicited SMS.
6. Maintain clear third-party trademark and merchant disclaimers, and don't claim endorsement or inventory not verified.
7. Consider general liability/cyber coverage and appropriate entity structure with an attorney/accountant.

## Domain, accounts and continuity
1. After clearance, secure a primary branded domain with auto-renew, registrar lock, 2FA, recovery controls and independent business email. Domain ownership is NOT trademark ownership.
2. Maintain separate owner/admin accounts, least privilege, recovery keys, code backups, incident plan and documented deploy rollback.
3. Verify exact legal entity and payment account match before contracts and App Store publisher setup.
4. Preserve public website continuity and redirects if brand changes.

## Current action gates
- **Do not file a trademark application, pay fees, purchase domains or represent a registration as granted without explicit authorization.**
- **Do not claim "Gift Finder" is exclusive.**
- **Do not change the public product name until distinctive alternative is cleared and approved.**
- Current legal protection status: preparation only; no verified filing, attorney review, or exclusive trademark rights.
