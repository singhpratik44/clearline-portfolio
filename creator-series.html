Players & coaches

Court to Code

Turn a drawn-up play into a scoreboard with your own scoring rule.

Read the floorBuild the scoreboard
  1. Pick “Help or Stay?” in Basketball IQ. Sketch both defensive choices and tell a partner which one you would reward.
  2. Open Backyard Scoreboard. Invent two scoring moves, give them different values and choose a winning score.
  3. Trade roles. Let a teammate try to jump past the winning score with one move. Fix the rule or explain why it holds.

Take away Two sketched plays, a scoring rule and the score from a teammate’s test.

Families & friends

Playable Postcards

Give someone a greeting they can play twice and finish two different ways.

Make a free greetingBuild the two endingsPrint a holiday thank-you postcard
  1. In Gifter, make a card for someone you know. Write one sentence that sounds like you. A nickname works.
  2. In Playable Postcards, turn the surprise into two choices. Write a different ending for each and try both.
  3. Place the greeting and the playable link side by side in your own message. Watch which choice your recipient tries first.

Take away A greeting and a replayable gift with two original endings.

Art clubs & makers

Gallery to Game

Borrow a compositional idea from an artwork and invent a maze another person can read.

Look through the galleryBuild a maze
  1. View an artwork in the APERTURE gallery. Trace with your finger how its shapes lead your eye. Sketch a new path of your own.
  2. In the Visual Coding Engine, select Maze Game and use Build map to place walls, coins and a finish along your route.
  3. Let a player run the maze. Ask where the route looked open but was blocked. Revise one tile and test again.

Take away An original playable maze and a before-and-after sketch of one revised tile.

Classrooms & science clubs

Rooftop Rain Lab

Test a grid rule, then use the same experiment method to decide where a rooftop tank should send its water.

Test rules on the gridModel the rooftop tank
  1. In the Visual Coding Engine, choose Science Grid and load its example. Predict what one step will change. Run it, then change the order of two rules and compare.
  2. Open Rooftop Water Lab. Set a starting supply and two route costs. Predict how much water will remain after each choice.
  3. Raise one route’s cost by one. Hold the supply fixed, repeat the run and show a partner which outcome changed.

Take away An original water-budget model and a pair of test results with just one input changed.

Families, libraries & story clubs

Radio Premiere

Write a mystery broadcast and let listeners choose which ending goes on air.

Write the broadcastHost a family arcade
  1. Use Midnight Newsroom to write one opening interruption. Give listeners two choices about which clue to follow.
  2. Make each route pay off with a different ending. Rehearse a short introduction that leaves both clues open.
  3. Use the family arcade playtest. One person hosts and another chooses the route. Ask which clue they remembered, then change a line based on what they say.

Take away A two-ending broadcast, a listener’s observation and one edited line.
