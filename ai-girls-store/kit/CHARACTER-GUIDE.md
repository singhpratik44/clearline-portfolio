# MUSE 01 — Character Guide

## NOVA / The future, made personal

**Fictional age:** 28. **Direction:** sharp, composed, curious. **Visual anchor:** a silver tailored jacket, dark sculptural hair, cool teal studio light, and precise architectural framing.

Nova works well as a creative reference for a technology concept, a futuristic editorial, a design presentation, or a fictional-world character board. Her story centers on making complicated ideas feel approachable. She notices one small detail before speaking and asks a useful question rather than presenting herself as infallible.

**Palette:** ink `#16282B`, silver `#C4CDD0`, teal `#4C9D9B`, pale mist `#E0EBE9`.

**Voice cues:** concise sentences, concrete observations, restrained warmth. Example fictional line: “Let's give the idea a shape we can actually use.” This is dialogue for a fictional character, not a quote from a real person.

**Keep stable:** adult appearance, face shape as shown in the reference, dark hair, clean tailoring, and cool lighting. **Vary:** camera distance, architectural setting, sleeve styling, and a single abstract prop. **Avoid:** brand logos, fabricated device capabilities, or implying she actually uses a product.

## AMARA / Warmth with intention

**Fictional age:** 30. **Direction:** grounded, observant, welcoming. **Visual anchor:** deep brown skin, cropped natural curls, terracotta tailoring, and warm sand-colored architecture.

Amara works well as a creative reference for lifestyle moodboards, interiors, design journals, or fictional editorial scenes. Her story centers on turning an everyday space into somewhere people want to spend time. She collects interesting textures and remembers why an object matters to its owner.

**Palette:** clay `#B86A4C`, espresso `#3E2723`, sand `#E9D4BA`, muted olive `#8B9273`.

**Voice cues:** sensory language, gentle precision, unhurried rhythm. Example fictional line: “A good space starts with how you want to feel in it.” Do not present fictional lines as customer testimonials.

**Keep stable:** adult appearance, face as shown in the reference, short natural curls, warm tailoring, tactile backgrounds. **Vary:** afternoon light, room composition, notebooks, ceramic objects, and plant forms. **Avoid:** fabricated wellness claims or implying professional credentials.

## LUNA / Make room for the unexpected

**Fictional age:** 27. **Direction:** imaginative, thoughtful, quietly playful. **Visual anchor:** long black hair, a violet knit top, a lavender studio, and soft directional light.

Luna works well as a creative reference for artistic projects, music moodboards, stationery concepts, or a fictional creator's world. Her story centers on collecting fragments—colors, sounds, unfinished drawings—and finding connections between them.

**Palette:** plum `#51445F`, lavender `#B3A0CF`, soft lilac `#E5DBEE`, warm white `#F1EBE4`.

**Voice cues:** vivid details, short reflective sentences, one unexpected image. Example fictional line: “The sketch was a little strange. That's why I kept it.”

**Keep stable:** adult appearance, face as shown in the reference, long dark hair, violet wardrobe, soft studio lighting. **Vary:** desk arrangement, sketchbook, abstract canvases, window placement, and a controlled accent color. **Avoid:** presenting AI images as photographs of a real artist or using real artists' signatures.

## Build a coherent five-image concept

1. **Choose a purpose.** Write one sentence: “This series helps [audience] understand [idea].” Avoid generating a large pile of unrelated images.
2. **Choose one character.** Keep the supplied portrait beside your working canvas. It is a reference, not a guarantee that another model will reproduce the exact face.
3. **Lock the essentials.** Reuse age, hair, wardrobe anchors, palette, and lighting. Keep a note of what you changed between generations.
4. **Change one variable at a time.** Start with portrait, half-body view, environment, object interaction, then wide composition. Approve each image before expanding.
5. **Edit for the final medium.** Use the actual dimensions required by your chosen placement. Leave room for copy. Avoid putting tiny text inside a generated image.
6. **Review before publication.** Check face continuity, hands, jewelry, reflections, unintended logos, text artifacts, and framing. Check that the image cannot be mistaken for a real testimonial or product endorsement.
7. **Record the source.** Keep the kit version, selected prompt, reference image, tool/model, and your edits. The supplied `characters.json` is a starting record.

## Scope of this kit

These are static portraits and creative planning materials. They are not production-ready 3D characters, Live2D files, motion capture, chatbot personas connected to a service, or exclusive identities. No sales or audience result is promised. You choose and review the final use.
